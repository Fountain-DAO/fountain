# Vision

## The problem

The internet is good at spreading claims and bad at settling them.

- Experts disagree, and readers can't tell who to trust.
- Fact-checkers and community notes rely on unpaid effort, and nobody has anything at stake.
- Prediction markets do put money behind beliefs. But they need an official source and a date to settle on, like an election result. Most of the questions that matter don't have one.
- Open problems in science and maths have no market at all. There's no way to put a price on an answer you want, or to get paid for finding one.

## The idea

Fountain combines two simple parts:

1. **A market maker** that sells shares in each possible outcome of a claim. Prices rise as people buy, so a price reads as the current consensus.
2. **A settlement game.** A claim settles when one outcome's price stays above a high threshold for long enough. There's no final backstop. If people keep disagreeing, the claim stays open.

The combination creates an incentive that matters more than either part alone. Shares can't be sold back to the market, so once you've staked on a claim, the only way to win is to bring others round to your view. That means publishing your best evidence and reasoning where everyone can check it. Every claim builds a public record of the arguments, who made them, and what they had at stake.

Fountain doesn't claim to find the truth. A settled claim means that the people with something at stake stopped disagreeing. That's a useful signal, not a guarantee, so the evidence stays attached and each result shows how contested it was.

## What it makes possible

- **Bounties for knowledge.** Anyone can put points behind a question they want answered: an open problem, a disputed result, a paper nobody has replicated. That funding is the bounty.
- **Rewards for finding answers.** If you know something the market doesn't, you can stake, publish, and collect once others are convinced. The reward grows with how much you moved the consensus and how unexpected the answer was. Confirming what everyone expected earns little. Overturning it earns the most.
- **Credit.** The record shows who found an answer first, and who funded the search for it.
- **A public good.** The arguments and evidence stay public, and anyone can read them and decide for themselves.

## Where we're starting

Science, maths and technology. In these areas evidence can be checked, communities already argue in public, and there's no shortage of open questions. Examples include:

- whether a result replicates
- whether an AI system really did what was claimed
- whether a proof holds

Politics and claims about private individuals are out of scope. Those fights are about more than evidence, and they bring risks a young system can't carry.

## AI and information markets

AI systems will soon make more claims than people can check. A market that pays whoever is right before everyone else is a natural check on those claims. It's also a natural benchmark for AI agents themselves, which can research a claim, post evidence and take a position like anyone else.

We believe markets that pay people for generating knowledge will become real economic infrastructure, for science, maths and AI. We don't think of Fountain as a game.

## Why points, for now

Today, most countries' law, Australia's included, treats staking money on a claim as gambling. We think that's the wrong category for information markets, but we follow the law as it stands. So Fountain runs on points.

Points have no monetary value, can't be bought, sold or transferred, and there is no plan to convert them into money or tokens. We hold them to a high standard anyway:

- the mechanism is published
- every point is accounted exactly
- every trade is public

If the law comes to recognise information markets, the same mechanism can carry real value. That's a long-term hope, not a promise about points.

## What Fountain is not

- **Not an oracle of truth.** It records consensus and the evidence behind it.
- **Not a casino.** It's for questions where evidence decides, not for sport or elections.
- **Not a token project.** There's no Fountain token.

## Principles

- Freedom of speech.
- Respect for open and civil debate.
- A commitment to seeking objective truth, through evidence.
- Honesty about uncertainty: a settled claim is a consensus, not a verdict.

## History

- **2020:** launched on Product Hunt as [Verifact](https://www.producthunt.com/products/verifact?launch=verifact), a hosted system where people staked points on claims, mostly about news and politics. It ran the original protocol, in points.
- **2021–2025:** rebranded as Fountain, through a few prototype iterations.
- **2026:** fountain.markets, a restart which puts the mechanism, a points economy and science, maths and technology first.
